Why GTA 6 Targets 30 FPS & How VRR 120Hz Displays Fix It

Rockstar's Heavy Simulation Pipeline:

GTA 6 runs advanced cloth physics, water simulation across the Keys, real-time AI crowd schedules, and ray-traced global illumination that stress the CPU architecture. Rockstar chose a stable, pristine 30 FPS target over dynamic 60 FPS compromises.

The 120Hz VRR Container Advantage:

If your TV supports HDMI 2.1 120Hz with Variable Refresh Rate (VRR), the console outputs at a 120Hz refresh interval. Each 30 FPS frame is refreshed exactly 4 times (40Hz/120Hz timing), eliminating standard 60Hz 3:2 pulldown stutter and making camera pans look remarkably fluid.

PSSR Upscaling on PS5 Pro:

On PlayStation 5 Pro, Rockstar leverages PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution (PSSR) machine-learning reconstruction. This preserves sharp edges and neon reflections in Downtown Vice City with virtually zero temporal ghosting.

Frequently Asked Questions: GTA 6 Settings

Troubleshooting input lag, TV calibrations, and stick deadzone configurations.

Why shouldn't I turn Motion Blur completely off at 30 FPS? +
At 30 frames per second, frames are displayed for 33.3 milliseconds each. Without slight shutter motion blur, quick camera rotations appear stepped and strobe-like (judder). Setting Motion Blur to Level 1 or 2 provides the ideal middle ground: smooth frame blending without smear.
What should I set my controller deadzones to? +
Start at 0.05. If your character or camera moves without touching the stick, incrementally increase it by 0.01 until the drift completely stops. Lower deadzones yield faster reaction times during high-stakes heist shootouts.
